\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \methodname, a topology-aware process reward framework that augments outcome-based RLVR with two verifiable structural signals: a topological structure reward evaluating DAG validity and a continuity reward enforcing inter-step referential coherence.
Both signals are computed by deterministic programs without learned reward models or human annotations.
Combined with Stratified Clipping Advantage Estimation (SCAE) to prevent reward hacking and reverse-KL distillation for model compression, \methodname improves mathematical reasoning accuracy while producing shorter, more structurally coherent chains compared to outcome-only GRPO baselines.
On 7{,}595 private critique problems, TopoPRM improves overall accuracy by $+13.4$ points over outcome-only GRPO while reducing average response length by 11\%.
The distilled 8B student achieves 82.5\% on GSM8K and 59.8\% on MATH-500, competitive with models of comparable scale.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our DAG extraction relies on rule-based parsing of mathematical expressions and textual markers, which may miss implicit logical dependencies or fail on non-standard notation.
The current reward design is tailored to structured mathematical critique tasks; extending it to free-form reasoning or non-mathematical domains would require domain-specific parsers.
The topology and continuity rewards capture structural properties but do not verify the mathematical correctness of individual derivation steps, which remains the responsibility of the outcome reward.
Finally, our evaluation focuses on Chinese mathematical critique and standard English benchmarks; cross-lingual generalisation has not been systematically studied.

\paragraph{Future Work.}
Several directions merit further investigation:
(i)~integrating learned PRMs~\citep{lightman2023lets} as complementary signals alongside our deterministic rewards to capture semantic reasoning quality;
(ii)~extending the DAG extraction to multi-modal reasoning traces that combine text, code, and diagrams;
(iii)~applying TopoPRM to other structured reasoning tasks such as theorem proving and scientific problem solving;
(iv)~exploring curriculum strategies that dynamically adjust the balance between topology and outcome rewards during training.
