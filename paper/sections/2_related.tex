\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards.}
RLHF~\citep{ouyang2022training} aligns language models with human
preferences through learned reward models.
DeepSeek-R1~\citep{guo2025deepseekr1} and
DeepSeekMath~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} replaced learned preference
models with programmatic correctness checks, establishing the
reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) paradigm for
mathematical reasoning.
GRPO~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} computes group-relative advantages
without a separate critic network, reducing memory cost relative to PPO.
\citet{mroueh2025grpo} provides a theoretical analysis showing that GRPO
with binary rewards can be expressed as a KL-regularised contrastive loss
with an explicit closed-form optimal policy.
$\lambda$-GRPO~\citep{lambdagrpo2025} introduces learnable token
preferences to address length bias in GRPO training.
These methods supervise only the final answer and treat the intermediate
reasoning trace as opaque.
TopoPRM retains the verifiable reward paradigm but extends it to
process-level supervision: the topology and continuity rewards are
computed by deterministic programs, yet they provide step-level structural
feedback that outcome-only RLVR cannot offer.

\paragraph{Process reward models.}
\citet{lightman2023lets} introduced process reward models (PRMs) that
assign scalar rewards to individual reasoning steps, using human
annotations of step-level correctness as training signal.
Math-Shepherd~\citep{wang2024mathshepherd} reduces annotation cost by
estimating step-level value through Monte Carlo rollouts from each
intermediate state.
Proof-RM~\citep{proofRM2025} targets mathematical proof verification and
designs reward functions that account for proof structure, using an
``LLM-as-a-RM-for-RM'' approach with balanced token weighting to
stabilise RL training.
Verifiable Process Reward Models (VPRMs)~\citep{vprm2025} introduce
deterministic, rule-based verifiers for intermediate reasoning steps in
medical evidence synthesis, achieving up to 20\% higher F1 than
state-of-the-art models.
PPRM~\citep{pprm2025} uses Bradley-Terry loss with preference-based
learning to mitigate bias from heuristic search strategies.
These approaches improve over outcome-only supervision but depend on
either expensive per-step human labels or learned verifiers whose accuracy
is itself uncertain.
TopoPRM takes a complementary approach: rather than scoring individual
steps, it extracts structural properties of the full reasoning graph
(acyclicity, connectivity, directional consistency) using deterministic
rules, requiring no annotations and introducing no additional model
parameters.

\paragraph{Graph-structured reasoning.}
Graph-of-Thought~\citep{besta2024graph} organises LLM reasoning into
graph structures through prompt engineering, enabling exploration of
multiple reasoning paths during inference.
GRP~\citep{grp2025} trains models to produce graph-structured outputs
with explicit cognitive labels, requiring specialised output formats and
task-specific decoding procedures.
TopoPRM is closest to this line but differs in two practical choices:
(1)~it keeps standard text generation format and extracts implicit DAGs
post-hoc, and (2)~it emphasises compatibility with stock RLVR training
stacks through plugin-level verifiable rewards.
This design avoids graph-annotation requirements and architecture changes
while retaining structure-aware optimisation signals.

\paragraph{Reasoning compression and distillation.}
Reasoning models produce thousands of tokens of internal deliberation,
much of which is redundant or even harmful~\citep{sang2026opsdc}.
On-policy self-distillation for reasoning compression
(OPSDC)~\citep{sang2026opsdc} teaches models to reason more concisely by
distilling their own concise behaviour, achieving 57--59\% token
reduction on MATH-500 while improving accuracy by 9--16 points.
MENTALMS~\citep{mentalms2025} trains models to reason in compressed
``Mentalese'' tokens, achieving 4--16$\times$ fewer tokens across
mathematical benchmarks.
\citet{compressionreasoning2026} benchmarks quantisation, distillation,
and pruning on reasoning models, finding that weight count impacts
knowledge memorisation more than reasoning capability.
Caprese~\citep{caprese2025} proposes low-rank distillation using
approximately 1\% additional parameters.
Standard reverse-KL distillation~\citep{hinton2015distilling} provides
the mode-seeking objective that encourages students to concentrate on
high-probability teacher modes.
TopoPRM's distillation stage differs from these approaches by using
\emph{process-aware filtering}: only teacher traces satisfying topology
and continuity quality thresholds are retained for student training,
ensuring that the compressed reasoning inherits structural quality rather
than merely shortening output length.

\paragraph{Reward aggregation and reward hacking.}
Reward hacking, where models exploit auxiliary reward signals without
genuine task improvement, is a persistent problem in RL-based
training~\citep{Denison2024SycophancyTS}.
DAPO~\citep{dapo2025} introduces dynamic sampling to stabilise training,
and difficulty-aware methods~\citep{difficultyGRPO2026} adjust
optimisation pressure based on problem difficulty.
TopoPRM addresses reward hacking through Stratified Clipping Advantage
Estimation (SCAE), which hierarchically prioritises answer correctness
over auxiliary process rewards, preventing models from accumulating
topology or continuity reward at the expense of accuracy.
