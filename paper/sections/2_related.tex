\paragraph{Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards and process supervision.}
RLHF~\citep{ouyang2022training} aligns language models with human preferences through learned reward models.
DeepSeek-R1~\citep{guo2025deepseekr1} and DeepSeekMath~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} replaced learned preference models with programmatic correctness checks, establishing the RLVR paradigm for mathematical reasoning.
GRPO~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} computes group-relative advantages without a separate critic network, reducing memory cost relative to PPO; \citet{mroueh2025grpo} showed that GRPO with binary rewards can be expressed as a KL-regularised contrastive loss with a closed-form optimal policy.
$\lambda$-GRPO~\citep{lambdagrpo2025} addresses length bias through learnable token preferences, and DAPO~\citep{dapo2025} introduces dynamic sampling to stabilise training.
These methods supervise only the final answer and treat intermediate reasoning as opaque.
On the process supervision side, \citet{lightman2023lets} introduced PRMs that assign scalar rewards to individual steps using human annotations, and Math-Shepherd~\citep{wang2024mathshepherd} reduced annotation cost via Monte Carlo rollout estimation.
Proof-RM~\citep{proofRM2025} designed structure-aware reward functions for proof verification, while VPRMs~\citep{vprm2025} applied deterministic rule-based verifiers to medical evidence synthesis.
Graph-of-Thought~\citep{besta2024graph} and GRP~\citep{grp2025} organise LLM reasoning into graph structures, but require specialised output formats or task-specific decoding.
TopoPRM differs from all of the above in that it extracts structural properties of the full reasoning graph using deterministic rules, requires no annotations or additional model parameters, and remains compatible with standard RLVR pipelines through plugin-level reward modules.

\paragraph{Reasoning compression and distillation.}
Long chain-of-thought reasoning produces thousands of tokens of internal deliberation, much of which is redundant.
OPSDC~\citep{sang2026opsdc} teaches models to reason more concisely through on-policy self-distillation, achieving 57--59\% token reduction on MATH-500 while improving accuracy.
MENTALMS~\citep{mentalms2025} trains models to reason in compressed tokens, and \citet{compressionreasoning2026} benchmarked quantisation, distillation, and pruning on reasoning models, finding that weight count affects knowledge memorisation more than reasoning capability.
Standard reverse-KL distillation~\citep{hinton2015distilling} provides the mode-seeking objective that encourages students to concentrate on high-probability teacher modes.
The distillation stage of TopoPRM differs from these approaches by using process-aware filtering: only teacher traces satisfying topology and continuity quality thresholds are retained for student training, ensuring that the compressed reasoning inherits structural quality rather than merely shortening output length.

\paragraph{Reward aggregation and reward hacking.}
Reward hacking, where models exploit auxiliary reward signals without genuine task improvement, is a persistent challenge in RL-based training~\citep{Denison2024SycophancyTS}.
Difficulty-aware methods~\citep{difficultyGRPO2026} adjust optimisation pressure based on problem difficulty.
TopoPRM addresses reward hacking through hierarchical reward aggregation, which uses answer correctness as a multiplicative base and process rewards as a controlled gain term, preventing models from accumulating topology or continuity reward at the expense of accuracy.
