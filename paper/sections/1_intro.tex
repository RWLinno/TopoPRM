\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Para 1: Background — RLVR landscape, core limitation
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a practical paradigm for improving mathematical reasoning in large language models~\citep{guo2025deepseekr1,shao2024deepseekmath}.
By replacing learned preference models with programmatic correctness checks, RLVR avoids the annotation cost and calibration burden of RLHF while retaining strong final-answer accuracy.
In most current deployments, however, the reward signal is dominated by outcome correctness, which leaves intermediate reasoning quality only weakly constrained.
Models trained under such sparse supervision may arrive at correct answers through structurally fragile traces, unsupported logical transitions, or unnecessarily verbose chains of thought~\citep{sang2026opsdc,mroueh2025grpo}.

% Para 2: Prior work on process supervision — what they did, what remains unsolved
Process reward models (PRMs) offer a natural remedy by providing dense, step-level feedback.
\citet{lightman2023lets} demonstrated that human-annotated step-level correctness labels substantially improve reasoning quality, and Math-Shepherd~\citep{wang2024mathshepherd} reduced annotation cost through Monte Carlo rollout estimation.
More recently, Proof-RM~\citep{proofRM2025} designed structure-aware reward functions for mathematical proof verification.
Despite these advances, learned PRMs introduce an additional model whose reliability must itself be calibrated, and annotation-based approaches scale poorly to new domains.
A complementary question therefore remains open: can process supervision be made deterministic, reproducible, and directly compatible with standard RLVR pipelines, without requiring a learned reward model or human step-level labels?

% Para 3: Our approach — TopoPRM
We address this question with TopoPRM, a framework built on two core ideas.
First, we introduce a verifiable process reward model that extracts an inferred dependency DAG from each reasoning trace using deterministic rules, then computes two complementary process rewards on this graph: a topology reward for global structural regularity (acyclicity, orphan-conclusion ratio, directional consistency) and a continuity reward for local step-to-step traceability.
Both rewards are verifiable in an operational sense: once a trace is generated, reward computation is reproducible, auditable, and programmatically checkable.
Second, we introduce reverse-KL reasoning distillation, which filters teacher traces by process-aware quality criteria and trains a smaller student via a mode-seeking reverse-KL objective, compressing graph-enriched teacher behaviour into compact chain-like student reasoning.
A hierarchical reward aggregation mechanism ensures that answer correctness remains the primary optimisation target while process signals provide within-stratum refinement.

% Para 4: Contributions
Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item We propose a deterministic verifiable process reward module that extracts dependency DAGs and computes topology and continuity rewards through deterministic programs, requiring no learned reward model or human annotation.
\item We introduce process-aware filtered distillation that compresses graph-enriched teacher behaviour into compact chain-like student reasoning via a mode-seeking reverse-KL objective.
\item We conduct systematic experiments organised around four research questions, demonstrating $+13.4$ point accuracy gains over outcome-only GRPO on private critique benchmarks, and competitive public benchmark performance (82.5\% GSM8K, 59.8\% MATH-500) from the distilled Qwen3-8B student.
\end{enumerate}
