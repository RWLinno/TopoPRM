\begin{abstract}
Reasoning traces in modern LLMs are rarely purely sequential: they branch into subgoals, reuse intermediate claims, and defer conclusions across steps. Outcome-only supervision ignores this latent structure, producing weak credit assignment on unsupported conclusions, spurious linearised chains, and redundant branches in long reasoning trajectories.
We propose \method, a topology-aware post-training framework that recovers an implicit dependency DAG from each trace and reuses one deterministic diagnostic pipeline for data construction, reward design, and distillation. \method combines correctness-first hierarchical reward aggregation with topology and continuity diagnostics; to keep auxiliary rewards from dominating the correctness signal, we follow prior work and estimate advantages via stratified clipping. The same diagnostics drive \framework (topology-guided self-distillation), where the topology reward acts as a dense token-level signal that compresses long traces while preserving dependency structure.
On public benchmarks, \method reaches 84.5\% accuracy on GSM8K with a 7B model under a unified evaluation protocol, and on in-domain validation data it improves accuracy by $+13.4$ points over outcome-only GRPO while reducing response length by 11\%. The framework uses no human step labels, no learned PRM verifier, and no special graph-generation format.
\end{abstract}
