\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate four aspects of \method. RQ1 tests whether its gains persist across model families and improve the accuracy--length frontier. RQ2 measures checkpoint sensitivity to removing topology or continuity rewards. RQ3 audits the extracted support edges against independent references. RQ4 provides a narrowly scoped boundary test against a learned semantic PRM. Appendix~\ref{app:stress_tests} reports matched-decoding, cross-backbone, structure--correctness, and extractor stress tests.

\subsection{Evaluation Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Training.}
All controlled reward variants start from the same Qwen3.5-9B Stage-I checkpoint~\citep{qwen35team2026}. Both stages use rank-64 LoRA adapters~\citep{hu2022lora}; Stage~II applies GRPO with two completions per prompt and a learning rate of $5\times10^{-6}$. The graph extractor remains frozen, and reference-solution graphs are not used because their step indices do not align with generated traces. Every structural reward is therefore computed from the rollout's own $\mathcal{G}_y$. Appendix~\ref{app:details} gives the complete configuration.
% 所有受控奖励变体均基于同一 Qwen3.5-9B 第一阶段检查点初始化。两个阶段均采用秩为 64 的 LoRA 适配器；第二阶段使用 GRPO 算法，每个提示对应两个生成结果，学习率设置为 $5\times10^{-6}$。图提取器保持冻结状态，且不使用参考解图，原因是其步骤索引与生成轨迹无法对齐。因此所有结构奖励均基于采样轨迹自身的 $\mathcal{G}_y$ 计算得出。完整配置详见附录~\ref{app:details}。

\subsection{Main Results: Accuracy and Efficiency (RQ1)}
\label{sec:rq1}

\paragraph{Observation 1: The archived ledger records the highest displayed average in three post-training families.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports macro-averages of 55.3, 58.3, and 60.9 for \method, respectively 8.9, 2.4, and 5.4 points above the strongest alternative in each family. \method is best or tied-best on five of nine tasks in every block, although the leading tasks vary across backbones. The 60.9 result is the highest displayed macro-average and is 10.2 points above the strongest complete-checkpoint reference. These are provisional within-ledger comparisons, not a cross-protocol SOTA claim, until the row-level provenance audit marked in the table is complete.

\paragraph{Observation 2: The complete reward improves both coordinates of the observed frontier.}
In the controlled pass@1 study, the complete reward is the only run on the observed accuracy--length frontier of every benchmark (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_frontier} and Table~\ref{tab:main_ablation}). Relative to outcome-only GRPO, it shortens responses by 24.0\%, 3.8\%, 13.5\%, and 4.5\% on GSM8K, MATH-500, OlympiadBench, and AIME~2024, while changing pass@1 by $+0.8$, $+0.2$, $+1.5$, and $+13.3$ points. GSM8K and MATH-500 exhibit primarily length reductions, whereas AIME exhibits the largest accuracy difference; Acc/kTok rises from 89.1 to 118.2 on GSM8K and from 6.6 to 12.3 on AIME, with smaller gains on the other two tasks. Since each variant is trained once, this is an observed checkpoint comparison rather than an estimate of training-seed uncertainty. The two ledgers are not pooled: one records provisional cross-family macro-averages, whereas the controlled suite compares checkpoint-level accuracy and length.

\subsection{Ablation Study (RQ2)}
\label{sec:rq2}

\paragraph{Observation 3: Checkpoint sensitivity differs by removed reward source.}
Removing topology changes GSM8K and MATH-500 by $-1.1$ and $0.0$ points, but changes OlympiadBench and AIME~2024 by $-3.0$ and $-10.0$ points while adding 700 and 129 tokens, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:main_ablation}). Removing continuity produces the larger observed degradation: pass@1 falls by 23.3--43.1 points, mean length rises from 1{,}612 to 2{,}046 tokens, and accuracy returns to or below Stage~I on three tasks. These differences are consistent with continuity and topology contributing distinct constraints, but they do not isolate either component causally: reward mass is redistributed, the no-continuity cap differs, and every configuration has one training run. Constructed reversals and cycle closures independently verify that the direction and acyclicity diagnostics respond to their intended graph violations (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:topology_counterfactuals}). The archived traces in Figure~\ref{fig:optimization_diagnostics} supply a separate within-suite view of optimization and compression; they are not pooled with Table~\ref{tab:main_ablation}.

\subsection{Auditing Recoverable Support (RQ3)}
\label{sec:rq3}

\input{tabs/edge_audit_main}

\paragraph{Observation 4: Two model references provide stable diagnostic labels, not proof certificates.}
Across 119 independently annotated traces, Qwen3-32B and Qwen2.5-32B agree on 89.8\% of 1{,}770 candidate pairs, with Cohen's $\kappa=0.734$ (Table~\ref{tab:edge_audit_main}). The annotators jointly label 368 pairs as support and dispute 181; their respective positive rates are 24.6\% and 27.2\%. Against Qwen3-32B, the released extractor obtains precision 0.478, recall 0.589, and F1 0.528; its F1 is 0.510 against Qwen2.5-32B, 0.508 against annotator consensus, and 0.528 against their union. This narrow range indicates stability across the two model references for diagnostic auditing. It does not justify interpreting the graph as a proof-level dependency parse, and model-generated references do not replace human adjudication.

The separate 120-trace typed ledger makes this boundary actionable. Its false positives include 145 variable-reference edges, compared with 34 expression-reference, 11 expression-overlap, 9 implicit-block, and 82 order-fallback edges. The dominant observed failure is therefore entity-role ambiguity rather than normalized expression reuse. This localization supports targeted calibration of ambiguous edges while retaining deterministic expression and claim matches with explicit provenance; Appendix~\ref{app:edge_cases} reports representative cases and the post-hoc rule sensitivity analysis.

That analysis illustrates the benefit and the limit of an auditable reward. A guarded revision raises precision from 0.480 to 0.580 on the same labels by tightening variable-only and fallback edges, but it also alters several extraction paths. Because the revision was designed after inspecting the audit sample, the increase is a post-hoc sensitivity result, not held-out evidence. A valid policy-level improvement requires fresh edge labels and retraining with the revised extractor.

\subsection{Boundary Analysis with a Semantic PRM (RQ4)}
\label{sec:rq4}

\input{tabs/prm_complementarity}

\paragraph{Observation 5: The hybrid yields a net gain of one correct MATH-500 item in the shared-pool test.}
On a common Best-of-8 candidate pool, multiplying the Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B score~\citep{zhang2025qwen25mathprm} by $1+q_{\mathrm{topo}}$ preserves 95.00\% GSM8K accuracy and changes MATH-500 from 70.00\% to 71.25\% (Table~\ref{tab:prm_complementarity}), increasing the number of correct selections by one at $n=80$. The full ledger in Appendix~\ref{app:stress_tests} gives the relevant boundary: topology-only selection is below the semantic PRM on both tasks, and the hybrid recovers only 1.25 of the 7.50 points between the PRM and pool oracle on MATH-500. Thus, the two scores can change aggregate accuracy under this fusion rule, but two 80-item pools cannot establish general complementarity. Larger pools, uncertainty intervals, and a prespecified fusion rule are required for a general Best-of-$N$ claim.

\subsection{Evidence Synthesis and Scope}

\paragraph{Evidence synthesis.}
The reported results connect downstream behavior to diagnostic consistency rather than to a verified causal mechanism. The archived ledger provisionally records the highest within-family macro-average in three post-training blocks, and the controlled suite places the complete reward on each observed accuracy--length frontier. Source-removal controls identify unequal checkpoint sensitivity consistent with the reward design, while constructed graph interventions and the edge audit show that the intermediate signal is inspectable and responds to targeted violations. The semantic-PRM comparison sets a further boundary: topology-only selection does not replace semantic scoring, and the hybrid produces a net gain of one correct MATH-500 item. Outcome, continuity, and topology therefore remain separately interpretable training signals, but the present experiments do not establish that any one signal is an interchangeable estimator of correctness or a proof certificate.

\paragraph{What auditability changes.}
The structural reward is useful here not because it certifies a derivation, but because every score can be decomposed into inspectable step pairs and evidence types. This changes the failure-analysis workflow. A low learned-verifier score identifies a suspicious trace but need not reveal which premise relation failed; the present ledger instead separates expression reuse, claim reuse, variable overlap, local transitions, and fallback order. The edge audit therefore points to a specific calibration target---ambiguous variable roles---while leaving stronger expression matches intact. The guarded extractor experiment demonstrates how that diagnosis can be translated into a candidate rule revision, and its post-hoc status makes the next validation step explicit: annotate fresh traces, freeze the revision, and retrain shared-initialization policies before claiming a downstream gain.

\paragraph{Where structural evidence should be bounded.}
Recoverable surface support is most credible when mathematical objects have stable symbolic anchors and intermediate claims are stated explicitly. It is less reliable for diagram-dependent geometry, paraphrase-heavy proofs, or traces that reuse a symbol for several semantic roles. In those settings, a sparse and well-formed graph may still encode an invalid deduction, while a correct solution may appear weakly connected because its premises are implicit. Outcome verification must therefore remain the correctness anchor. A learned edge scorer, semantic PRM, or formal checker can complement the deterministic ledger, but each changes the supervision contract: learned scorers trade provenance for coverage, and formal systems require a representation that ordinary free-form traces do not supply. The appropriate interpretation of \method is thus an auditable training interface between scalar outcome rewards and fully semantic verification.

\paragraph{Operational implication.}
This interface is confined to training. Graph construction and pair scoring shape the policy update, but the deployed model generates ordinary text without invoking the extractor or a separate verifier. The distinction matters for both efficiency and evaluation: inference cost is unchanged, whereas training cost and extractor quality must be assessed separately. It also makes revisions modular. Improvements to segmentation or ambiguous-edge handling can be audited before they enter policy training, while the outcome term and decoding interface remain fixed. The present experiments validate this workflow at the diagnostic level; they do not yet show that every successful extractor revision transfers to a better policy.

\paragraph{Stress tests delimit the claim.}
The appendix reports three additional checks under their complete protocols. A matched DR1-Qwen-7B rerun yields no stable accuracy ordering among outcome-only, outcome-plus-length, and \method under repeated stochastic decoding. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, \method shortens GSM8K and MATH-500 responses but preserves accuracy only on GSM8K. Higher topology-score strata are also more accurate, yet outcome-only training exhibits a comparable stratification gap. These results bound the evidence to the displayed cross-family comparison, the observed Qwen3.5-9B frontier, audited recoverable support, and the shared-pool hybrid observation. They do not establish backbone-independent gains, semantic verification, or formal proof recovery. A mechanism-focused follow-up should stratify examples by dependency depth and selector disagreement, and combine multi-seed training with human-adjudicated edges and larger candidate pools.

\subsection{Stress Tests and Scope}

The appendix reports three additional checks under their full protocols. In a matched DR1-Qwen-7B rerun, repeated stochastic decoding gives no stable accuracy ordering among outcome-only, outcome-plus-length, and \method. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, \method shortens responses on GSM8K and MATH-500 while preserving accuracy only on GSM8K. Finally, higher topology-score strata are associated with higher correctness, but a comparable stratification gap also appears for outcome-only training. These checks restrict the claim to the displayed cross-family benchmark result, the observed Qwen3.5-9B frontier, audited recoverable support, and the reported hybrid-selection observation. They do not establish universal backbone gains, semantic verification, or formal proof recovery.
% 附录按照完整实验流程补充报告了三项验证结果。在匹配的DR1-Qwen-7B重跑实验中，重复随机解码在仅基于结果、结果加长度、以及\method三者之间未得到稳定的准确率排序。在Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct模型上，\method缩短了GSM8K和MATH-500数据集上的响应长度，但仅在GSM8K上保持了准确率。最后，拓扑得分层级越高，正确率也越高，但仅基于结果的训练也存在类似的层级差距。上述验证将结论限定于已展示的跨模型族基准测试结果、观测到的Qwen3.5-9B前沿性能、经审核可复现的支撑依据，以及报告的混合选择观测结果。这些验证并未证明该方法能在所有骨干模型上通用增益、实现语义验证，或是完成形式化证明复现。
