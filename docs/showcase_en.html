❓ Motivation

Current RLVR training relies solely on final-answer correctness, leaving three fundamental gaps:

🔗 Fragile Reasoning Structure

Models may reach correct answers through logical jumps, circular dependencies, or unsupported conclusions. Intermediate steps lack effective supervision.

📉 Reward Collapse

Naive weighted-sum aggregation of multi-source rewards drives batch reward variance to zero, preventing GRPO from distinguishing good and bad samples. Measured: 93% of batches have zero reward std under linear aggregation.

📏 Reasoning Redundancy

LLM reasoning traces often exceed 1,000 tokens with redundant derivations and dead-end explorations, wasting inference resources.

🏗️ Method Overview

TopoPRM comprises three components: multi-source reward module, post-training pipeline, and reverse-KL distillation.

Hierarchical Reward Aggregation

Rbase = wo · Routcome + wf · Rformat + wl · Rlength

Rtotal = Rbase × (1 + α · R̃topo + (1−α) · R̃cont)

Process rewards act as a multiplicative gain on the base correctness signal, preserving answer-correctness primacy while amplifying structural quality differences.

🔬 RQ2: Ablation Study

Topology Is the Most Impactful Signal

Removing topology reward causes the largest drop (-9.9 points), confirming global DAG-level structural regularity is the most impactful process signal.

Continuity Provides Complementary Value

Removing continuity yields a smaller but meaningful drop (-5.9 points), validating local step-to-step traceability.

Reward Collapse Analysis

Linear aggregation: 93.8% of batches have zero reward std.
Clipped: 80.0%.
Hierarchical: effectively resolves collapse.

🎓 RQ4: Distillation

Reverse-KL distillation with process-aware filtering: student surpasses teacher

Student Surpasses Teacher

Qwen3-8B student achieves 59.0% overall, far exceeding the 32B teacher (29.7%). Process-aware filtering removes noisy training signal.

Cross-Lingual Generalisation

Trained exclusively on Chinese critique data, the student achieves 82.5% on GSM8K and 59.8% on MATH-500, competitive with same-scale models.

LRKL = Ex~D[ DKL( πS(·|x) ‖ πT(·|x) ) ]

Reverse KL is mode-seeking: it encourages the student to concentrate on high-probability teacher modes rather than averaging over diverse outputs, compressing graph-enriched teacher reasoning into compact chain-like student reasoning.
