\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
We conduct extensive experiments to evaluate our methods on nine benchmarks spanning mathematical reasoning and general tasks: \texttt{GSM8K}, \texttt{MATH-500}, \texttt{Olympiad}, \texttt{Omni-MATH}, \texttt{AIME 2024}, \texttt{AIME 2025}, \texttt{CNMO 2024}, \texttt{MMLU}, \texttt{GPQA-D}, comparing against more than ten baselines including reference open-source models.
Among them, we adopt Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen2.5-7B~\citep{yang2024qwen2_5}, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B~\citep{deepseekr1} as the primary base models. 
Training variants use: (i) LoRA SFT~\citep{vonwerra2022trl,hu2022lora} on $\sim$19.5K DAG-annotated math traces, with 3 epochs, 64 rank, learning rate $5{\times}10^{-6}$ and $\alpha{=}128$.
GRPO is run for 300 steps, 4 generations/prompt with learning rate $5{\times}10^{-6}$, $\beta{=}0.04$, equipped with outcome-only reward or the hierarchical TopoPRM reward (Eq.~\ref{eq:r_total}).
The hybrid topology extractor combines rule templates with batched LLM dependency inference and is invoked asynchronously alongside rollout generation (cost analysis in §\ref{sec:rq3}).
The primary metric is accuracy, while pass@k / maj@5 / token-efficiency are recorded for completeness~\citep{wang2023com}. Full setting and implementation details are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:details}.

\subsection{Main Results (RQ1)}
\label{sec:rq1}

\paragraph{Unified Accuracy Evaluation.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_accuracy} reports pass@5 accuracy across nine benchmarks for three backbone families. TopoPRM achieves the highest average accuracy (55.3\%, 58.3\%, and 60.9\%) among all training variants on the three base models, respectively.
% On Qwen3.5-9B, The gains concentrate on elementary 
% and general benchmarks (GSM8K: 97.9 vs.\ 93.3; MMLU: 86.4 vs.\ 63.0).
On Qwen2.5-7B, TopoPRM exceeds the GRPO baseline on 7 of 9 benchmarks and improves MMLU by $+$20.2 points (82.7 vs.\ 62.5), indicating that topology supervision transfers to general reasoning without task-specific tuning. TopoPRM also surpasses the strong SimpleRL-Zoo baseline on 5 of 9 benchmarks (Olympiad: 47.8 vs.\ 38.6; Omni: 57.2 vs.\ 40.4; MMLU: 82.7 vs.\ 62.5) while remaining competitive elsewhere.

\paragraph{Shorter Chains, but Higher Efficiency.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_detailed} further decomposes performance by token cost.
TopoPRM (Full) achieves the highest Acc/kTok on all four primary benchmarks (GSM8K: 118.2, MATH-500: 35.1, OlympiadBench: 18.6, Omni-MATH: 26.6), generating up to 24\% fewer tokens on GSM8K and about 13\% fewer on the four-primary-benchmark mean than outcome-only GRPO while maintaining equal or higher accuracy.
This pattern---higher accuracy at lower token cost---is the signature of structurally tighter reasoning chains, where topology supervision prunes redundant derivation branches.

\paragraph{Reward Component Contribution.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_compact} isolates the contribution of each reward component on Qwen3.5-9B across 8 public benchmarks.
Removing continuity causes the largest degradation ($\Delta{=}{-}29.3$ points), with Collapse\% rising from 37.9\% to 68.8\%, indicating that local step-traceability provides the primary gradient signal for GRPO.
Removing topology or ACE each causes a moderate drop ($\Delta{\approx}{-}3.8$, Collapse\% at 42--45\%), while outcome-only GRPO shows a $-$4.7 point deficit with 57.8\% collapse.
The full model also achieves the shortest generation (1,692 tokens) and highest token-efficiency (26.9 Acc/kTok), suggesting that topology-aware supervision prunes structurally redundant branches rather than merely penalising length.
At larger capacity (a pre-experiment used Qwen-32B in Appendix~\ref{appx:pre_exp}), the dominant signal shifts from continuity to topology.

\paragraph{Training Dynamics.}
Figure~\ref{fig:training_dynamics} visualizes the training metrics trajectory over 300 steps. Standard GRPO gradually compresses mean completion length from 3,700 to 1,200 tokens, whereas TopoPRM remains below 500 tokens throughout training---over $2.4\times$ shorter at convergence---while sitting on a high reward plateau from step~0 with strictly positive reward std. The w/o-continuity variant converges to a higher nominal reward but with near-zero reward std (bottom panel), indicating mode collapse: the model produces a single output pattern that scores well on the remaining reward components but lacks diversity for continued learning.

\paragraph{Accuracy per Token.}
Figure~\ref{fig:efficiency} summarizes the token-efficiency trade-off on four public benchmarks.
TopoPRM (Full) achieves 48.1 Acc/kTok at 1,568 tokens, while outcome-only GRPO reaches 37.9 at 1,737 tokens, and the w/o-continuity variant collapses to 14.0 at 2,046 tokens.
The monotonic degradation confirms that topology and continuity contribute complementary efficiency gains: global structure prevents redundant branching, while local continuity prevents unsupported transitions that inflate length.
A detailed analysis on our in-domain critique set is in Appendix~\ref{appx:pre_exp}, Table~\ref{tab:ablation_length}.


\paragraph{Training Cost.}
Full TopoPRM post-training on 8$\times$A100-80GB GPUs completes in ${\sim}$30 GPU-hours, including SFT takes ${\sim}$3h, GRPO ${\sim}$15h, and TGSD ${\sim}$12h. DAG extraction runs on rule-based and LLM generation mixedly in parallel, adding negligible wall-clock overhead. %By contrast, learned PRM baselines such as Math-Shepherd require 50+ GPU-hours to train the verifier alone before it can be used as a reward signal.

\paragraph{Topology-Guided Cross-Scale Distillation.}
Table~\ref{tab:compression} evaluates the \framework stage, which uses the 9B TopoPRM teacher (Stage-II output) to distil cross-scale students via topology-guided on-policy distillation.
The 4B student retains 98.2\% of the teacher's GSM8K accuracy (82.8 vs.\ 84.3) and 92.3\% on MATH-500 (61.5 vs.\ 66.6) while reducing token usage to 0.56$\times$ and preserving 93\% structural retention (acyclicity, edge coverage).
Scaling down to 2B and 0.8B yields graceful degradation: accuracy drops 5--11 points per halving of parameters, while structural retention remains above 81\%.
The SFT-distill baseline at 4B achieves lower accuracy (79.4 GSM8K) with worse structural retention (0.86$\times$) despite generating longer outputs (438 vs.\ 382 tokens), confirming that topology-guided on-policy sampling produces a higher-quality training signal than static teacher traces.
The off-policy KL baseline is generated at the token cap (4096) with only 0.41$\times$ structural retention, leaving the student with near-zero usable supervision (details in Appendix~\ref{appx:tgsd_details}).
Cross-scale ablation results on our in-domain validation set (Appendix~\ref{appx:pre_exp}) further show that topology rewards are most effective at larger model scales (32B: $\Delta{=}{-}13.0$ for outcome-only), where the model has sufficient capacity to produce structurally coherent DAGs.
