\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Implementation Details.}
We adopt DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B~\citep{guo2025deepseekr1} as the primary base model for its strong reasoning capabilities, and additionally validate on Qwen3.5-9B to confirm method transferability.
Training uses HuggingFace TRL~\citep{vonwerra2022trl} with LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} (rank~64, $\alpha{=}128$, all linear layers).
SFT runs for 3~epochs with learning rate $5{\times}10^{-5}$ on ${\sim}19{,}500$ public DAG-annotated math samples constructed from GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} and MATH~\citep{hendrycks2021math} training splits.
GRPO training runs for 200 gradient steps with 4 generations per prompt, group size~4, learning rate $5{\times}10^{-6}$, and KL coefficient $\beta{=}0.04$, using the hierarchical TopoPRM reward (Eq.~\ref{eq:r_total}).
All experiments use 2$\times$ NVIDIA L20X GPUs (143\,GB each).
Full hyperparameter details are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:details}.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
For the private in-domain setting, we evaluate on two Chinese critique
sets: a \emph{Middle School} set (2{,}181 samples) and a \emph{High
School} set (5{,}414 samples), totalling 7{,}595 evaluation samples.
For the public suite, we evaluate on
\texttt{GSM8K}~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k},
\texttt{MATH-500}~\citep{hendrycks2021math},
\texttt{AIME\,2024}, \texttt{CNMO\,2024}, and \texttt{MMLU}.
All public evaluations use a unified \texttt{transformers}-backend script
with each model's native chat template applied (\texttt{tokenizer.apply\_chat\_template}),
shared answer extraction, and shared matching.
Math benchmarks draw $k{=}5$ completions per item (sampling with temperature~$0.7$);
we report pass@1, maj@5, and prm@5 in Table~\ref{tab:public_benchmark}.
The prm@$k$ column selects the candidate with the highest DAG topology + continuity score,
directly demonstrating the value of our process reward at inference time.


\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against the following systems:
\emph{(i)}~DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B zero-shot baseline (chat template, no fine-tuning);
\emph{(ii)}~+ SFT (LoRA fine-tuned on ${\sim}19{,}500$ DAG-annotated math samples);
\emph{(iii)}~+ GRPO with outcome-only reward (no topology/continuity signals);
\emph{(iv)}~two ablation variants that remove the topology or continuity reward channel;
\emph{(v)}~external references including DeepSeek-R1 671B, Qwen3-32B, QwQ-32B, and LIMO for public benchmark positioning.
We additionally report a Qwen3.5-9B ablation suite (Appendix~\ref{app:details}) to confirm method transferability.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
On private sets we report Score-Acc (critique scoring accuracy), Error-F1
(F1 for error step identification), Format\% (structural compliance with
the required \texttt{<think>}$\cdots$\texttt{<answer>} format), and Avg-Len
(mean prediction length in tokens).
On public benchmarks we report three complementary metrics: \textbf{pass@1} (single-sample accuracy), \textbf{maj@5} (majority vote over 5 samples), and \textbf{prm@5} (reranking by DAG topology + continuity score).
The prm@$k$ metric directly demonstrates the value of our process reward at inference time without any additional training.
For structural analysis we additionally report DAG-derived diagnostics
(acyclicity, orphan-conclusion ratio, directional consistency).

\subsection{RQ1: Effectiveness of TopoPRM}
\label{sec:rq1}

\input{tables/private_results}

\paragraph{Private critique benchmarks.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_private} summarises results on the private critique benchmarks.
Among GRPO variants, TopoPRM (full) achieves the highest overall critique accuracy at 29.7\%, with 37.2\% on Middle-School and 22.3\% on High-School subsets.
This corresponds to a $+13.4$ absolute improvement over outcome-only GRPO (16.3\%) and a $+9.9$ gain over the topology-ablated variant (19.8\%).
TopoPRM simultaneously achieves the highest format compliance among GRPO models (94.6\% Middle, 89.3\% High) while producing the shortest responses (364 tokens Middle, 424 tokens High), indicating that structure-aware process rewards improve both quality and efficiency.

The SFT baseline achieves comparable raw accuracy on Middle-School (29.0\%) but with substantially lower format compliance (78.5\% Middle, 65.0\% High) and much longer responses (1\,164 tokens), confirming that SFT learns content patterns without internalising the structured output format.
GRPO training with TopoPRM rewards surpasses SFT accuracy on Middle-School ($+8.2$ points) while raising format compliance by $+16.1$ percentage points and cutting response length by 69\%.

\input{tables/public_results}

\paragraph{Public math benchmarks.}
Table~\ref{tab:public_benchmark} reports pass@1, maj@5, and prm@5 on public math benchmarks.
The prm@5 column directly demonstrates the value of our DAG-based process reward:
by selecting the candidate with the highest topology + continuity score among 5 samples,
prm@5 consistently outperforms both pass@1 and maj@5, validating that
topological structure provides a stronger reranking signal than majority vote alone.
Table~\ref{tab:dag_structural_public} reports DAG structural quality on MATH-500,
confirming that TopoPRM training produces reasoning traces with higher acyclicity,
fewer orphan conclusions, and richer dependency structure.

\subsection{RQ2: Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:rq2}

\input{tables/ablation_reward}

\paragraph{Reward component ablation.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_reward} isolates the contribution of each reward component.
Removing the topology reward causes the largest degradation ($\Delta = -9.9$), confirming that global DAG-level structural regularity is the most impactful signal.
Removing continuity yields a smaller but meaningful drop ($\Delta = -5.9$), showing complementary value from local step-to-step traceability.
The outcome-only baseline suffers the largest deficit ($\Delta = -13.4$), validating the necessity of process-level supervision beyond answer correctness.
All GRPO variants achieve substantially higher format compliance ($\geq$89\%) than SFT (78.5\%), suggesting that on-policy optimisation reinforces format adherence even when process rewards are partially ablated.

\input{tables/aggregation_ablation}

\paragraph{Aggregation strategy ablation.}
Table~\ref{tab:aggregation_ablation} compares reward aggregation strategies.
Naive linear aggregation leads to severe reward collapse: after approximately 20 gradient steps, the fraction of batches with zero reward standard deviation exceeds 93\%, and the reward standard deviation drops below $10^{-3}$, preventing GRPO from computing meaningful group-relative advantages.
The hierarchical multiplicative formulation (Eq.~\ref{eq:r_total}) resolves this by treating process rewards as a multiplicative gain on the base correctness signal, maintaining reward variance throughout training.
Clipped linear aggregation partially mitigates collapse but underperforms the hierarchical variant.

\input{tables/structural_metrics}

\paragraph{Structural quality.}
Table~\ref{tab:structural_metrics} reports DAG-level structural diagnostics.
Models trained with the full TopoPRM reward produce traces with higher acyclicity rates, fewer orphan conclusions, and stronger directional consistency.
Improvements in these metrics reflect enhanced structural traceability under the extracted-graph proxy, not proof-level semantic verification---an important scope distinction discussed in \S\ref{sec:reward_module}.
After introducing sentence-level claim filtering and hybrid verdict assignment, structural diagnostics become substantially more informative:
the fraction of samples with all-\texttt{unverifiable} nodes drops sharply, and edge-source statistics show reduced sequential-edge domination.
This confirms that the observed gains are not artifacts of a chain-like fallback graph, but are driven by richer dependency structures.

\input{tables/ablation_cross_scale}

\paragraph{Cross-scale transferability.}
\label{sec:analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_cross_scale} extends the ablation to Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen2.5-7B.
On 32B, every reward component contributes positively: removing topology costs $-9.5$ points and removing continuity costs $-5.5$.
However, the pattern \emph{reverses} on smaller models: on 9B, removing topology \emph{improves} accuracy by $+2.3$; on 7B, outcome-only GRPO outperforms the full TopoPRM by $+5.8$.

Training-dynamics diagnostics reveal three failure modes.
\emph{(i)~Truncation noise}: on 9B, 100\% of generations are truncated at \texttt{max\_completion\_length}, producing incomplete DAGs whose topology scores inject noise rather than signal.
\emph{(ii)~Reward-variance collapse}: on 7B, ablation rewards yield zero within-batch reward standard deviation (ZeroStd$\approx$1.0) for 99\% of batches, preventing GRPO from computing meaningful advantages.
Only the hierarchical multiplicative formulation maintains non-zero variance (ZeroStd$=$0.017) via batch-level min-max rescaling.
\emph{(iii)~Format collapse}: 7B format compliance drops to 19--52\% (vs.\ 83--91\% on 9B/32B), breaking the outcome reward's ability to parse answers.

\paragraph{Extended context mitigates truncation noise.}
To address failure mode~(i), we increase \texttt{max\_completion\_length} from 1024 to 4096 on 9B.
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_cross_scale} (bottom block) shows that extended context narrows the gap between reward variants: no-topology achieves 30.5\% overall (vs.\ 31.0\% at mcl=1024), while gated and outcome-only both reach 29.0\%.
Critically, the hierarchical variant drops to 27.5\%, suggesting that the multiplicative formulation amplifies noise from longer but still imperfect DAGs.
The gated reward, which applies topology scoring only when the base format+outcome signal exceeds a threshold, achieves parity with outcome-only (29.0\%), confirming that selective topology application avoids noise injection.

\paragraph{SFT initialisation matters.}
We observe that 9B models initialised from a 32B-distilled SFT adapter versus a native 9B SFT adapter exhibit different GRPO dynamics.
The 9B SFT baseline achieves 40.5\% Middle accuracy with 98.0\% format compliance, substantially outperforming all GRPO variants (29.5--33.0\% Middle).
This suggests that for smaller models, supervised fine-tuning on high-quality critique data may be more effective than reinforcement learning with process rewards, particularly when the model lacks sufficient capacity to simultaneously learn format, content, and structural reasoning.

Increasing the number of generations from 2 to 4 (ng4) recovers $+2.3$ points on 9B, matching the no-topology ablation, suggesting that richer exploration partially compensates for noisy topology signals.
These results indicate that topology-aware process rewards require sufficient model capacity and generation budget to be effective, and motivate future work on truncation-robust topology scoring and adaptive reward curricula.

\paragraph{Layer-compression closure.}
To verify the end-to-end topology-to-compression pipeline, we additionally evaluate layer-wise DAG compression, where each topological layer is collapsed into one chain node.
Compared with direct sequential-chain baselines, this produces a shorter reasoning backbone while preserving inter-layer ordering constraints.
In practice, the layer-compressed chain provides a stable interface for distillation-data filtering and visual auditing, which connects process-reward diagnostics directly to reasoning-compression behavior.

\subsection{RQ3: Efficiency Analysis}
\label{sec:rq3}

\input{tables/ablation_length}

\paragraph{Accuracy--length trade-off.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_length} examines the accuracy--length trade-off.
TopoPRM achieves 75.5 accuracy points per 1\,000 tokens, the highest efficiency ratio among all configurations.
Compared with outcome-only GRPO, TopoPRM reduces mean response length by 11.2\% while improving accuracy by $+13.4$ points, suggesting that deterministic process supervision discourages redundant reasoning branches and unsupported logical jumps.
The base Qwen3-32B model produces responses $3.3\times$ longer than TopoPRM while achieving less than half the accuracy.

\paragraph{Training cost.}
All GRPO experiments use 8$\times$ GPUs with LoRA (rank 64).
The full TopoPRM training (SFT + GRPO) completes in approximately 12 GPU-hours.
The reward computation overhead from DAG extraction and topology scoring adds $<$5\% wall-clock time relative to outcome-only GRPO, as the deterministic extractor operates on CPU in parallel with GPU-bound generation.

\subsection{RQ5: Reasoning Compression via TVSD ($\le$4B)}
\label{sec:rq4}

\paragraph{Framing: what success looks like.}
The question this RQ answers is \emph{not} ``can the 4B student beat the
9B teacher on accuracy?''---a smaller student cannot exceed the teacher's
capacity ceiling, so chasing accuracy parity would mis-state the
contribution. Instead we ask two measurable questions:
(i)~does the student \emph{retain} the teacher's structural reasoning
profile (Acyclic\%, No-Orphan\%, Edge-Keep\%)?
(ii)~does it \emph{halve} the generation budget at inference with only a
mild (1--3~pp) absolute drop on math benchmarks?
This is a Pareto-frontier claim along the accuracy--tokens axis, not a
leaderboard claim.

\input{tables/compression}

\paragraph{Accuracy--length Pareto.}
Table~\ref{tab:compression} reports the four quantities that matter for
this RQ side by side: math pass@1, average output tokens, tokens ratio
relative to the 9B teacher, and structural-retention metrics.
Under the unified \texttt{transformers}-backend protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}), the SFT-baseline 4B student already shortens
responses by 45--55\% relative to the 9B teacher on
\texttt{gsm8k}/\texttt{math500}/\texttt{olympiadbench}/\texttt{omni\_math},
with accuracy drops in the expected 1--3~pp band on those four math-only
benchmarks. Note that on adversarial long-CoT benchmarks
(\texttt{aime2024}/\texttt{aime2025}/\texttt{cnmo2024}) the 4B student
drops more sharply; those are outside the scope of a compression claim
and we discuss them explicitly as a limitation
(Section~\ref{app:limitations}).

\paragraph{Why not use the legacy RKL student as the compression number.}
Our initial reverse-KL distillation (32B teacher $\to$ 8B student)
produced a student with pass@1 $=$ 81.0\% on GSM8K and 45.4\% on
MATH-500, with every rollout hitting the 2048-token generation cap.
Post-mortem (Section~\ref{sec:compression}) revealed that only 0.4\%
of the 32B teacher traces closed the required \texttt{<answer>} tag
within the training budget, so the RKL student never observed a
stopping pattern; it is therefore the \emph{opposite} of a
compression result---it is a length-collapse result. We keep this
row in Table~\ref{tab:compression} as the anti-example that motivates
TVSD's on-policy, format-closed design.

\input{tables/revision_gain}

\paragraph{Revision gain as a compression probe.}
Table~\ref{tab:revision_gain} reports a Generate-then-Revise diagnostic:
for each problem we sample one first-attempt response, then prompt the
same model to produce a revision conditioned on
$(r_{\mathrm{out}},r_{\mathrm{topo}})$ via $P_r$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:topo_pr}).
The topology-verified revision prompt delivers a larger gain than an
outcome-only revision prompt built on the same checkpoint: in
particular, the TVSD-conditioned revision shortens structurally-correct
but verbose traces without reducing accuracy, which is exactly the
behaviour that transfers to $\pi_S$ in Phase~III-B.

\input{tables/case_study}

\paragraph{Case study.}
Table~\ref{tab:case_study} gives representative qualitative examples.
The compact student preserves the key DAG-level logical nodes of the
teacher (the conclusion and its supporting premises are identified
consistently) while eliminating redundant exploration branches. This
matches the quantitative structural-retention numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:compression} and is the empirical content behind our
compression claim.
