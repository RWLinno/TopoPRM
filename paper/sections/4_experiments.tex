\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Base model and training.}
We use Qwen3-32B as the base model and MS-Swift~\citep{msswift} for both SFT and GRPO training.
We choose GRPO over DPO because our supervision takes the form of deterministic process scores rather than pairwise preference labels, and GRPO directly optimizes sampled trajectories under such reward signals~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,mroueh2025grpo}.
The teacher is trained with the full TopoPRM composite reward (topology + continuity + correctness-first shaping).
Distillation experiments compress the teacher into a Qwen3-8B student via process-filtered reverse-KL training.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate on two complementary benchmark families:
\emph{(i)}~a private Chinese mathematical critique dataset comprising Middle-School (2\,181 problems) and High-School (5\,414 problems) subsets, totalling 7\,595 samples; and
\emph{(ii)}~public benchmarks including GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} (1\,319 problems) and MATH-500~\citep{hendrycks2021math} (500 problems).
Private sets are used for process-level critique evaluation; public sets provide broad positioning on final-answer accuracy.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
On private sets we report \emph{Score-Acc} (critique scoring accuracy), \emph{Format\%} (structural compliance with the required \texttt{<think>}$\cdots$\texttt{<answer>} format), and \emph{Avg-Len} (mean prediction length in tokens).
On public benchmarks we report standard accuracy.
For structural analysis we additionally report DAG-derived diagnostics (acyclicity, orphan-conclusion ratio, directional consistency).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against: (1)~Qwen3-32B base (zero-shot), (2)~Qwen3-32B + SFT, (3)~outcome-only GRPO, and (4)~two ablation variants that remove topology or continuity reward channels.
External references (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) are included for public benchmark positioning only.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:main_results}

\input{tables/main_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} summarises the main results.
Among GRPO variants, TopoPRM (full) achieves the highest overall critique accuracy at 29.7\%, with 37.2\% on Middle-School and 22.3\% on High-School subsets.
This represents a $+13.4$ absolute improvement over outcome-only GRPO (16.3\%) and a $+9.9$ gain over the topology-ablated variant (19.8\%).
Notably, TopoPRM simultaneously achieves the highest format compliance among GRPO models (94.6\% Middle, 89.3\% High) while producing the shortest responses (364 tokens Middle, 424 tokens High), demonstrating that structure-aware process rewards improve both quality and efficiency.

The SFT baseline achieves higher raw accuracy (32.8\%) but with substantially lower format compliance (48.8\%), indicating that SFT learns content patterns without internalising the structured output format.
GRPO training with TopoPRM rewards closes this accuracy gap while raising format compliance by $+45.8$ percentage points.

The distilled Qwen3-8B student achieves a striking 59.0\% overall accuracy---surpassing the 32B teacher---with near-perfect format compliance (94--97\%).
We attribute this to the process-aware filtering in distillation data construction, which selects only high-quality teacher traces, effectively removing noisy training signal.

\subsection{Ablation on Verifiable PRM Components}
\label{sec:ablation}

\input{tables/ablation_reward}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation_reward} isolates the contribution of each reward component.
Removing the topology reward causes the largest degradation ($\Delta = -9.9$), confirming that global DAG-level structural regularity is the most impactful signal.
Removing continuity yields a smaller but meaningful drop ($\Delta = -5.9$), showing complementary value from local step-to-step traceability.
The outcome-only baseline suffers the largest deficit ($\Delta = -13.4$), validating the necessity of process-level supervision beyond answer correctness.

Interestingly, all GRPO variants achieve substantially higher format compliance ($\geq$89\%) than SFT (48.8\%), suggesting that GRPO's on-policy optimisation naturally reinforces format adherence through the format reward channel, even when process rewards are partially ablated.

\subsection{Structural Quality Analysis}
\label{sec:structural_quality}

\input{tables/structural_metrics}

Table~\ref{tab:structural_metrics} reports DAG-level structural diagnostics.
Models trained with the full TopoPRM reward are expected to produce traces with higher acyclicity rates, fewer orphan conclusions, and stronger directional consistency.
We interpret improvements in these metrics as enhanced structural traceability under the extracted-graph proxy, not as proof-level semantic verification---an important scope distinction discussed in \S\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Accuracy--Length Trade-off}
\label{sec:length_analysis}

\input{tables/ablation_length}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation_length} examines the accuracy--length trade-off.
TopoPRM achieves the best efficiency ratio (75.5 accuracy points per 1\,000 tokens) among all configurations.
Compared with outcome-only GRPO, TopoPRM reduces mean response length by 11.2\% while improving accuracy by $+13.4$ points, suggesting that deterministic process supervision discourages redundant reasoning branches and unsupported logical jumps.
The base Qwen3-32B model produces responses $3.3\times$ longer than TopoPRM while achieving less than half the accuracy, highlighting the efficiency gains from structured RLVR training.

\paragraph{Reward collapse analysis.}
We observe that naive linear reward aggregation leads to severe reward collapse during GRPO training: after approximately 20 gradient steps, the fraction of batches with zero reward standard deviation exceeds 93\%, and the reward standard deviation drops below $10^{-3}$.
This prevents GRPO from computing meaningful group-relative advantages.
The hierarchical multiplicative aggregation (Eq.~\ref{eq:r_total}) with batch rescaling and anti-collapse mechanisms resolves this issue by maintaining reward variance throughout training.

\subsection{Reverse-KL Distillation Results}
\label{sec:compression_results}

\input{tables/compression}

Table~\ref{tab:compression} evaluates reasoning compression via reverse-KL distillation.
The Qwen3-8B student not only retains but substantially \emph{exceeds} the teacher's critique accuracy (59.0\% vs.\ 29.7\%), while maintaining high format compliance.
On public benchmarks, the student achieves 82.5\% on GSM8K (full set, 1\,319 problems) and 59.8\% on MATH-500, competitive with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (84.5\% / 52.2\%) despite being trained exclusively on Chinese critique data.

We note that the teacher's anomalously low MATH-500 score (9.2\%) is likely an artefact of answer-extraction mismatch with Qwen3-32B's thinking-mode output format, rather than a genuine capability deficit.
The student's strong MATH-500 performance (59.8\%) confirms that the distillation process successfully transfers mathematical reasoning capability while compressing the reasoning chain.
